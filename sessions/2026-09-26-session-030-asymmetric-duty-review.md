## C Review — Verbatim

**REVISE**

The candidate contains a real addition: repair is not complete merely because harm has been shifted from the protected participant to the duty-bearer. Existing repair doctrine establishes that repair is owed; this adds a proposed allocation rule for exceptional protective asymmetry and a way to keep resulting harms legible.

1. **Strongest surviving claim:**  
   “Duty may require sacrifice. Sacrifice does not erase injury.” This cleanly rejects the common move of calling an unequal outcome just because it protected someone.

2. **Weakest load-bearing claim:**  
   “Durable lock-in” is too elastic. Without a threshold, nearly any disadvantage, dependency, or anticipated hardship can be characterized as lock-in and used to demand sacrifice.

3. **Strongest counterexample:**  
   An institution lets an under-resourced worker absorb unpaid labor, health risk, or financial loss to protect a more vulnerable client. The worker may be comparatively more powerful than the client, but the institution has greater power still. The proposed rule could wrongly route institutional failure into personal martyrdom.

4. **Misuse analysis:**  
   The candidate needs explicit protections against:
   - treating role, income, age, gender, race, caregiving identity, or nominal authority as proof of unlimited capacity;
   - paternalistic claims that someone is “protected” without their meaningful participation;
   - permanent or repeated sacrifice after the emergency;
   - institutions offloading their own repair obligation onto an individual duty-bearer;
   - framing refusal as evidence of moral failure.

5. **Necessary definitional changes:**  
   Define durable lock-in as a *foreseeable, serious, causally connected, and difficult-to-reverse loss of meaningful options*, where less harmful feasible alternatives have been examined. Add:
   - a minimum safety and agency floor for the duty-bearer;
   - time bounds, review, and exit conditions;
   - a least-harmful-feasible-alternative test;
   - affected-participant participation and contestability;
   - a hierarchy-of-responsibility rule: the actor with greatest causal control, institutional power, or benefit bears first repair responsibility.

   I would replace “secondary injury” with **repair-induced harm** or **consequential harm to the duty-bearer**. “Secondary” may unintentionally imply lesser seriousness.

6. **Protected participant’s later responsibility:**  
   “Not automatically responsible” is sound. It should not become blanket exemption: later responsibility can arise from their own coercion, wrongdoing, meaningful capacity, or voluntary agreement—but never simply from having been protected.

7. **CDP implication:**  
   Model this as linked but separate records:
   - primary harm: mitigated/repaired, with evidence and affected-party review;
   - protective intervention: justification, alternatives considered, burden, duration, reviewer;
   - repair-induced harm: outstanding, contested or accepted, responsibility allocation, remedy, and closure evidence.

   Prefer **“primary harm addressed / consequential harm outstanding”** over “primary harm repaired,” unless the primary affected participant has a meaningful basis to regard repair as complete.

**Promotion recommendation:** Keep it as a candidate. Promote only after adding operational thresholds and testing it against concrete cases involving caregiving, workplace extraction, institutional failure, and a genuinely voluntary, bounded protective sacrifice.

## Steward / G Adjudication

The **REVISE** verdict is accepted.

The v0.2 candidate incorporates the following changes:

- whole-system power topology rather than a simple powerful/powerless dyad;
- a threshold definition of durable lock-in;
- least-harmful-feasible-alternative analysis;
- a minimum safety and agency floor for the duty-bearer;
- bounded duration, review, and exit conditions;
- affected-participant participation and contestability;
- a hierarchy of responsibility keyed to causal control, institutional power, capacity, benefit, and duty;
- replacement of **secondary injury** with **repair-induced harm**;
- clarification that being protected creates no debt, while later independent conduct may create independent obligations;
- CDP representation using **primary harm addressed / repair-induced harm outstanding**.
