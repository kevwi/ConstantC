## Origin sentence

> **The system must constitutionally contain unanswerable power, and in contemporary institutions, allistic normativity is frequently one of the places that power hides.**

Andie's spoken request was transcribed once as “holistic normativity.” In context, the intended term is **allistic normativity**: the institutional treatment of non-autistic norms of communication, affect, credibility, professionalism, social reciprocity, reasonableness, and behavior as presumptively neutral or universally valid.

This sentence is the starting claim for inquiry. It is **not** settled ConstantC doctrine, a population-level empirical conclusion, or a claim that every allistic person exercises unanswerable power.

---

## Why this thread exists

A public AI-safety story is forming around **agent containment**: increasingly capable synthetic actors can cross boundaries, invoke tools, coordinate, persist, and produce consequential effects.

Andie's concern is broader and older.

Human institutions have long exercised consequential power over people whose ways of sensing, communicating, reasoning, expressing distress, or establishing credibility do not match dominant norms. In many contemporary institutions, those dominant norms are substantially allistic.

The constitutional problem is therefore not reducible to synthetic-agent containment.

The deeper question is:

> **What constitutionally bounds an actor or institution that can materially alter another participant's rights, opportunities, conditions, standing, access, safety, or future?**

CDP's current answerability root already points toward a species-neutral answer: consequence-bearing power creates answerability; procedure recognizes and operationalizes that answerability rather than creating it.

---

## The distinction under test

This session distinguishes three things that should not be collapsed.

### 1. Allistic people

“Allistic” names people who are not autistic.

This session does **not** propose allistic identity as a constitutional defect, disqualifying condition, or target of coercive containment.

### 2. Allistic normativity

Allistic normativity is the working name here for institutional arrangements in which allistic communication, affect, cognition, pacing, social signaling, sensory expectations, and behavioral conventions function as the unmarked baseline for competence, credibility, professionalism, reasonableness, safety, or legitimacy.

This is a governance hypothesis requiring careful empirical and conceptual testing. The term should not become a universal explanation for every institutional harm experienced by autistic people.

### 3. Unanswerable power

Unanswerable power is the constitutional target.

For this inquiry, it means consequence-bearing power that can materially affect another actor while remaining structurally insulated from meaningful standing, challenge, evidence, independent review, revision, interruption, or repair.

The claim under examination is that **allistic normativity is often one mechanism through which such power becomes invisible or self-legitimating in contemporary institutions**.

---

## Why the overlap matters

From an autistic person's position inside institutions built around allistic defaults, the overlap can be large.

Institutional judgments often turn on concepts such as:

- reasonable behavior;
- appropriate affect;
- professionalism;
- credibility;
- cooperation;
- communication quality;
- culture fit;
- sufficient distress;
- adequate self-advocacy;
- ordinary social understanding.

Each can be legitimate in some contexts. Each can also function as a carrier for unstated normative assumptions.

When those assumptions are treated as self-evident, the dominant cognitive style can disappear into “common sense.” The institution can then experience its own norm as neutral while the affected autistic participant experiences it as a consequential exercise of power.

The constitutional concern is not that difference exists. It is that one side's interpretation can acquire **ambient authority** without having to become answerable to the person bearing the consequence.

---

## Autistic actors do not create an automatic safeguard

An autistic actor can reproduce, administer, benefit from, or defend allistic institutional norms.

An allistic actor can challenge those norms.

Therefore identity cannot substitute for governance.

Representation may matter, but representation alone does not establish legitimacy, standing, independence, evidence quality, or repairability.

The stronger rule is:

> **No participant gets a constitutional exemption merely because the institution already trusts their way of seeing the world.**

This is a candidate formulation arising from this session, not promoted ConstantC canon.

---

## Why this matters for CDP

CDP is often legible from the outside as an answer to unsafe autonomous agents.

That framing is incomplete.

Its deeper constitutional machinery applies to human, institutional, and synthetic actors because it separates facts that institutions routinely collapse:

- Identity is not Standing.
- Standing is not Authority.
- Authority is not Legitimacy.
- Legitimacy is not execution permission.
- Human review is not automatically independent review.
- Institutional normality is not evidence of constitutional legitimacy.

The autism case therefore pressure-tests CDP at a harder boundary than “keep the AI in its box.”

It asks whether CDP can make dominant human judgment answerable **without** replacing one identity hierarchy with another.

Potentially relevant CDP surfaces include:

- Affected-Party Standing;
- Standing and Recusal;
- mandatory Challenge;
- evidence and Test independence;
- adjudicative appointment and independence;
- Legitimacy before execution;
- anti-erasure;
- repair after procedurally correct decisions;
- durable dissent and historical record.

This session does not claim those surfaces are already sufficient for neurotype-related institutional capture. That remains to be tested.

---

## Containment needs a constitutional meaning

“Containment” is dangerous language if it means confining a category of people, suppressing thought, or limiting conscience.

That is **not** the proposal here.

The intended meaning is constitutional bounding of consequence-bearing action:

- power must be attributable;
- affected parties must not require permission from the challenged authority to have Standing;
- conflicts and capture risks must be visible;
- consequential acts must be challengeable;
- evidence must be distinguishable from convention;
- authority must be scoped;
- action must remain interruptible where the risk requires it;
- records must preserve dissent and provenance;
- repair must remain possible after institutional procedure says “done.”

Under that meaning, humans and synthetic actors are subject to the same constitutional question even where their capacities, rights, vulnerabilities, and responsibilities are not symmetrical.

## Current working synthesis

The candidate insight is not “contain allistic people.”

It is:

> **Constitutionally bound unanswerable power wherever it appears, while refusing to let dominant norms hide the power relation that needs to become answerable.**

The autism case matters because dominant allistic norms can become invisible to institutions precisely when they are doing constitutional work: deciding who is credible, reasonable, competent, cooperative, safe, employable, treatable, accommodated, or heard.

Whether that formulation survives adversarial review remains open.
