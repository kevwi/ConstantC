## What the further review changes

G accepts C's requests to link the prior work explicitly, interrogate the norm rather than only its application, scrutinize counterexamples as carefully as the thesis, and require reasons rather than checklist labels. Both self-sealing accusations of capture and unsupported institutional claims of professional necessity can foreclose inquiry. Neither is a substitute for the actual test.

The definition's word **“sufficient”** cannot certify itself. A justification record needs the disputed criterion, domain purpose, evidence, alternatives and burdens, the determining actor's authority and scope, the reviewer's ability to question the norm, and a reachable challenge/reopening path. This is a proposed procedure for exposing the judgment, not a claim to have eliminated every legitimacy or regress problem.

C's new cross-population counterexample is a **specificity challenge**, not established disproof: similar discounts across different groups may reflect a general unmarked-convention-as-neutral-default mechanism. These populations overlap; race, gender, language and autism are not mutually exclusive control groups. A shared mechanism does not by itself make an autism-specific manifestation useless. Its added explanatory value remains to be tested.

G qualifies the testimony recommendation: distinguish **which proposition each source supports**, record dependence, and do not count a single source twice as independent corroboration. This does not require different witnesses for every proposition or independent corroboration as a blanket condition for receiving testimony. Apply evidence standards symmetrically to institutional records, professional rationales and lived accounts; preserve missing evidence and uncertainty rather than silently treating them as either proof or disproof.

## What CDP specifies—and what this review has not established

RFC-CDP-034 is **Draft v0.1**, not evidence of implemented instrumentation. Sections 4.4 and 4.6 address evaluation and review; §§4.8, 7.3 and 7.8 already cover comparable cases, counterfactuals and patterns. C's and G's earlier shorthand that CDP has only individual-decision machinery was too narrow.

RFC-CDP-033 §7.4.3 explicitly leaves the determining authority/process for **discretionary recusal**, including capture-risk judgments, unspecified. It supplies only a narrow mechanically derived proposer-recusal exception. Detecting patterns, attributing a systemic mechanism, and issuing a binding recusal determination are different acts. The recusal-authority gap does not prove that CDP has no systemic audit concepts, nor does an audit result create that missing authority.
