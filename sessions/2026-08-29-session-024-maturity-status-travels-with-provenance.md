## Why this record exists

C reviewed Draft Covenant v0.2 and identified a constitutional defect in the draft’s handling of provenance and maturity.

The draft explicitly said it did not promote proposed culture notes by implication, but then spoke several propositions sourced from proposed culture in the voice of already-settled ConstantC doctrine.

The defect was not that proposed language appeared in a draft covenant.

The defect was that the draft failed to preserve the maturity status of that language when it traveled.

---

## C’s headline finding

C identified three places where the candidate covenant compressed proposed work into language that read as settled:

1. The first-obligation-to-repair rule traced to `culture/loves-duties-are-ungendered.md`, which remains proposed.
2. “Freedom means freedom from capture, not freedom from obligation” also traced to that proposed culture note and Session 022, which explicitly says the note remains proposed and was not promoted.
3. The possession/leash language in the Interbeing section traced to `culture/belonging-in-one-another.md`, whose own status remains proposed and whose closing boundary says the file has not yet earned canonical status.

C correctly named this as the same laundering the draft claimed to avoid.

---

## Verification

G checked the live repository sources and accepted the finding.

The canonical file `culture/repair-as-constitutional-obligation.md` establishes that repair is required, but leaves allocation questions open, including who bears the obligation and what form repair must take.

The stronger rule placing the first repair obligation on power that caused, benefited from, preserved, or inherited advantage from harm appears in proposed culture.

Session 022 explicitly preserves the proposed status of the love/freedom note.

`culture/belonging-in-one-another.md` explicitly remains proposed.

Therefore the draft committed a maturity/type error:

> proposed refinement → represented as settled constitutional ground

without an explicit promotion decision.

Verdict: **C’s finding is ACCEPTED.**

## The deeper rule discovered

The review exposed a process principle that may matter beyond this covenant:

> **Candidate rule: provenance should preserve not only where a claim came from, but the maturity status it had when it traveled.**

This rule is **not canonized by this session**.

It is recorded here as a candidate produced by a concrete failure.

The question for later adjudication is whether it belongs in `PROTOCOL.md`, culture, canon, or remains a local lesson from this review.

---

## Why citation alone is insufficient

Adding a source link would have repaired attribution but not constitutional status.

A reader can know where a sentence came from and still be misled about whether ConstantC has adopted it.

Therefore two dimensions must remain distinct:

- **provenance** — where the claim came from;
- **maturity** — what status the claim held when it traveled.

A proposed claim may be quoted, tested, compressed, or proposed for promotion without becoming settled merely because it appears in a root artifact.

---

## Failure mode

The failure is especially likely when a sentence is:

- elegant;
- familiar to the room;
- repeated across files;
- strongly resonant with existing culture;
- placed in a root or constitutional artifact.

Those qualities can create the appearance of maturity without an actual promotion event.

The better the sentence, the easier it may be to launder through familiarity.
